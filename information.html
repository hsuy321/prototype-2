1 Person, Nature consists of a series of public interventions, involving the placement of stock imagery into 34 newsracks around New York City.

The practice of stock photography began at the beginning of the 20th century with newspapers and magazines, as a means to cut costs in comparison to hiring staff or commission-based photographers. Nowadays, stock photography has been distributed and integrated into many aspects surrounding our everyday lives, from healthcare ads to supermarket banners.

This project reinterprets the stock image in the context of Guy Debord’s Spectacle, the theory that our everyday experiences are mediated by images, commodities, and the mass media.1 The stock image is a staged representation of everyday life, shot with the intention of having “maximum stock impact”, an image that can be easily sold, edited, and used by corporations. The stock image also anticipates the spectator’s reaction to the image prior to the fact, and the spectator inherently accepts the message of the stock image.3

1 Guy Debord, The Society of the Spectacle, Thesis 4.
2 Megan Garber, The Tao of Shutterstock: What Makes a Stock Photo a Stock Photo?
3 Guy Debord, The Society of the Spectacle, Thesis 30.

All stock imagery used in this project were retrieved from the search term “1 Person, Nature”, which returns a series of optimistic images of people in nature seemingly detached from the Spectacle, far from the influence of image, text, and other elements of mass media. However, the same optimism in the imagery will inevitably become commodified to sell anything from a flight ticket to a pill.

Map, Checklist, Information

This exhibition consists of a growing series of public interventions placing stock photography into newsracks around New York City.

Stock photography was popularized in the 1920s, when newspapers, magazines, and advertisers began purchasing ready-made images as a more affordable and efficient alternative to commissioned photography.1 Today, stock photography is built into the visual language of everyday life, from physical posters to digital advertisements.

1 “Stock Photography,” Wikipedia.

When we see a stock image, the agency does not lie within ourselves but in the image itself, the metadata titles and search categories that define the context of the image in use. This can be referred back to the ideas proposed by Guy Debord in 1967’s Society of the Spectacle, where he refers to “a social relation between people that is mediated by images.”2 The agency lies in a staged representation of everyday life, reinterpreting common situations, interactions, and emotions into a photograph deemed for “maximum stock impact.”3 In 2012, The Atlantic even described stock photography as “becoming its own specialized skill,” in light of the growing demand and needs of stock image users, leading photographers to shoot images optimized for being easily usable and editable.

2 Guy Debord, The Society of the Spectacle, Thesis 4.

3 Megan Garber, The Tao of Shutterstock: What Makes a Stock Photo a Stock Photo?, The Atlantic, 2012.

The perceived quality of the stock image is paradoxical. In the case of the image seen through the newsrack window, the viewer does not recognize the qualities that make the image a stock image, but the headlines and narratives that are attached to the image.4 And for the newsrack publication in circulation, the image becomes recognizable in its repetition as well. Hito Steyerl’s concept of the “poor image” is a useful comparison, an image that degrades in resolution as a result of its own mass circulation.5 While the ideal stock image is high quality, the meanings of the stock image similarly become degraded as it is circulated, the newsrack being a source of a constant bombardment of information.

4 Guy Debord, The Society of the Spectacle, Thesis 30.

5 Hito Steyerl, In Defense of the Poor Image, e-flux journal, 2009.

This exhibition presents what remains when the stock image is reduced to itself, creating a new relationship between image and viewer.
